Cusco sits at about 3,400 metres (verify). Your lungs will notice before you do. Most visitors feel *something* in the first 24 hours: a dull headache, a racing heart on the stairs, a night of patchy sleep. Very fit people get it too. Fitness and altitude tolerance are different things.

<Callout>This is general guidance, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before you travel, especially if you have a heart or lung condition, are pregnant, or are travelling with young children.</Callout>

## 1. Sleep lower first

The single most useful thing we do is change the order. Instead of flying into Cusco and sleeping there, we drive you straight down to the **Sacred Valley**, roughly 2,800–2,900 metres (verify). It's about 500 metres lower than the city and a far gentler first night. Two nights there, then Machu Picchu (lower again, about 2,400 m), and *then* Cusco.

By the time you arrive in the city, your body has had four or five days to adjust.

## 2. Take day one seriously

On your first day above 2,500 metres:

- Walk slowly, especially uphill and on stairs.
- Skip the big hike, the late night and the third pisco sour.
- Eat light and drink plenty of water.
- Rest in the afternoon, even if you feel fine.

## 3. Climb in steps

Mountain guides talk about *climb high, sleep low*. If you're heading somewhere very high, like Rainbow Mountain (about 5,000 m) or the Salkantay Pass (about 4,600 m), do it at the end of your Andean days, never the start.

Our [altitude page](/altitude) shows how each journey climbs, night by night. You can also [build your own route](/altitude#route-builder) and see where it gets steep.

## 4. Know the warning signs

Mild symptoms usually ease within a day or two. Tell your guide straight away if symptoms get worse, or if you notice confusion, breathlessness at rest, or difficulty walking in a straight line. The answer is usually to go lower, and our team can arrange that at any hour.

## 5. About coca tea and medication

Many hotels offer *mate de coca* on arrival. It's a traditional mild remedy and pleasant to drink, but it doesn't replace acclimatising. Some travellers take prescription medication for altitude; whether that's right for you is a conversation for your doctor, not for us.

## The short version

Start lower, go slowly, drink water and leave the biggest climbs for later. Then Cusco is all yours.
